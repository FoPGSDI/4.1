# Tidally-Deformed NS Solutions

Up until now we have concentrated on isolated NSs in the slow-rotation approximation. We will now switch gears and consider NSs in a binary system. We focus on one of the binary components, the primary, and study how it is tidally deformed by its companion, assuming the primary is not spinning. One can construct tidally-deformed NS solutions in a manner similar to the construction of slowly-rotating solutions. In both cases, the deformation (either due to rotation or tidal effects) is treated as a small deformation away from sphericity.

## Einstein Equations and Exterior Solutions

The leading-order effect of tidal perturbations enters at $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2)$. This is because this effect is generated by an electric tidal perturbation, which must be of even parity. Moreover, in this section we are interested in non-rotating tidally deformed NSs, so we can set $\omega_1=0$ in Eqs. (Eq. m2)--(Eq. h2R). By eliminating $m_2$ and $K_2$ from these 3 equations, one obtains a master equation for $h_2$ [hinderer-love]:

$$\begin{aligned}

0=& \frac{d^2 h_2}{dR^2} + \left\{ \frac{2}{R} + \left[ \frac{2M}{R^2} + 4 \pi R (p-\rho) \right] e^{\lambda} \right\} \frac{dh_2}{dR}  \\
-& \left\{ \frac{6 e^{\lambda}}{R^2} - 4\pi \left[ 5 \rho + 9p + (\rho+p) \frac{d\rho}{dp} \right] e^{\lambda} + \left( \frac{d\nu}{dR} \right)^2 \right\} h_2\,.
\end{aligned}$$

The observable related to tidally-deformed NS will eventually be a tidal Love number, and thus, we will need to asymptotically expand the exterior solution in the buffer zone. This time, however, the radius of curvature that defines the buffer zone is related to the tidal field generated by the companion. This radius is approximately equal to the orbital separation of the binary. Therefore, when solving Eq. (Eq. h2RR) in the exterior region, one cannot impose asymptotic flatness to eliminate one of the constants of integration. Keeping this in mind, the solution to the above equation is [hinderer-love]

$$\begin{aligned}
h_2^\mathrm{ext} &=& c_1 \left( \frac{R}{M_*} \right)^2 \left( 1-\frac{2M_*}{R} \right)  \\
& & \times \left[ - \frac{2M_* (R-M_*) (3 R^2 - 6 M_* R - 2 M_*^2)}{R^2 (R-2M_*)^2} \right.  \\
& & \left. + 3 \ln \left( \frac{R}{R-2M_*} \right) \right] +c_2 \left( \frac{R}{M_*} \right)^2 \left( 1-\frac{2M_*}{R} \right)\,,  \\

\end{aligned}$$

where $c_1$ and $c_2$ are integration constants.

## Interior Solutions and the Tidal Love Number

The interior solution to Eq. (Eq. h2RR) can be obtained by solving this equation numerically with the initial condition in Eq. (Eq. h20) and its derivative. We obtain this numerical solution in the same way as we obtained $h_{2}$ for slowly-rotating NSs. As before, the interior solution will depend on the integration constant $B$, which, in principle, is determined by matching this solution to the exterior solution in Eq. (Eq. h2-ext-tides) at the NS surface:

$$
h_2^\mathrm{int} (\mathcal{R}_*) = h_2^\mathrm{ext} (\mathcal{R}_*), \quad h_2’{}^\mathrm{int} (\mathcal{R}_*) = h_2’{}^\mathrm{ext} (\mathcal{R}_*)\,.

$$

Notice that by using Eq. (Eq. h2-tidal-matching), we can re-express $c_1$ and $c_2$ in terms of $h_2(\mathcal{R}_*)$, $h_2’(\mathcal{R}_*)$ and the NS compactness $C$.

With the interior solution in hand, let us now define the tidal Love number. As in the case of the rotational Love number, the tidal one characterizes the deformability of a NS away from sphericity, but this time due to the presence of a tidal field induced by a companion. In the buffer zone, the $(t,t)$ component of the metric takes the form of Eq. (Eq. tidal-asymp), but with $Q^\mathrm{(rot)} \to Q^{\mathrm{(tid)}}$ and $\mathcal{E}^\mathrm{(rot)} \to {\cal{E}}^{\mathrm{(tid)}}$, where $Q^\mathrm{(tid)}$ and ${\cal{E}}^{\mathrm{(tid)}}$ correspond to the tidally-induced quadrupole moment and the tidal potential, induced by the companion. We then define the tidal Love number $\lambda^\mathrm{(tid)}$ by

$$\begin{aligned}
\lambda^\mathrm{(tid)} & \equiv & - \frac{Q^\mathrm{(tid)}}{\mathcal{E}^\mathrm{(tid)}}\,,

\end{aligned}$$

and its dimensionless versions $k_2^\mathrm{(tid)}$ and $\bar{\lambda}^\mathrm{(tid)}$ by

$$\begin{aligned}
k_2^\mathrm{(tid)} &\equiv & \frac{3}{2} \frac{\lambda^\mathrm{(tid)}}{\mathcal{R}_*^5}\,, \\
\bar{\lambda}^\mathrm{(tid)} &\equiv & \frac{\lambda^\mathrm{(tid)}}{M_*^5} = \frac{2}{3} k_2^\mathrm{(tid)} C^{-5}\,.

\end{aligned}$$

Following [flanagan-hinderer-love], we here refer to $\lambda^\mathrm{(tid)}$ as the tidal Love number[^1] and we refer to $k_2^\mathrm{(tid)}$ and $\bar{\lambda}^\mathrm{(tid)}$ as the tidal apsidal constant and dimensionless tidal Love number, respectively.

![](lambdaM-PRD.eps)

The prescription of the tidal Love number is completed by finding the value of ${\cal{E}}^{\mathrm{(tid)}}$, which is determined by the asymptotic behavior of $h_{2}$ in the buffer zone. Taylor-expanding this quantity in the buffer zone, one finds [hinderer-love]

$$
h_2^\mathrm{ext} = \frac{16}{5} c_1 \frac{M_*^3}{R^3} + c_2 \frac{R^2}{M_*^2} + \mathcal{O}\left( \frac{M_*^4}{R^4}, \frac{R}{M_*} \right)\,.
$$

As shown in Eq. (Eq. tidal-asymp), the term in the asymptotic expansion of $g_{tt}$ (or $h_{2}$) in the buffer zone that is proportional to $R^{-3}$ gives us the tidal quadrupole moment, while the term proportional to $R^{2}$ gives us the tidally-induced electric quadrupole tidal tensor. Thus, we find that $c_1$ is related to $Q^{\mathrm{(tid)}}$, while $c_2$ is related to ${\cal{E}}^{\mathrm{(tid)}}$.

The tidal apsidal constant can then be found by taking the ratio of $c_1$ and $c_2$ [hinderer-love]:

$$\begin{aligned}

k_2^\mathrm{(tid)} &= \frac{8}{5} C^5 \frac{c_{1}}{c_{2}} = \frac{8}{5} C^5 (1-2 C)^2[2+2C(y-1)-y]  \\
&\times  \left\{ 2C[6-3y+3C(5y-8)] \right.  \\
&\left. +4C^3[13-11y+C(3y-2)+2C^2(1+y)] \right.  \\
& \left. +3(1-2C)^2[2-y+2C(y-1)] \ln(1-2C)  \right\}^{-1}\,,
\end{aligned}$$

with $y\equiv \mathcal{R}_* h_2’(\mathcal{R}_*)/h_2(\mathcal{R}_*)$. In the second equality, we have rewritten $c_{1,2}$ in terms of $h_{2}$, its derivative and the NS compactness.

We see then that the tidal apsidal constant only depends on $y$, which simplifies the way one must solve Eq. (Eq. h2RR). First, we notice that Eq. (Eq. h2RR) is a homogeneous equation for $h_{2}$, and thus, the integration constant $B$ in Eq. (Eq. h20) only changes the solution $h_2$ by a constant factor. Since $y \propto h_{2}’/h_{2}$ does not depend on this overall factor, it suffices to solve Eq. (Eq. h2RR) with an arbitrary test value for $B$, if one is only interested in the tidal apsidal constant. We have calculated the tidal apsidal constant, as well as the tidal Love number for a sequence of stars with varying $M_{*}$ and $C$. We have found that our results agree exactly with Figs. 1 and 2 of [hinderer-lackey-lang-read].

Figure [ref:fig:lambdaMC] shows the dimensionless Love number $\bar{\lambda}^\mathrm{(tid)}$ as  functions of $M_*$ and $C$. Observe that the $\bar{\lambda}^\mathrm{(tid)}$ curves for realistic EoSs approach each other as $C$ increases, and moreover, they approach the BH limit as $C \to 0.5$. Once more, as before, the BH limit cannot be taken from the sequence of NS considered, as there is no finite central density that would lead to BH formation.

[^1]: In some references, $\lambda^\mathrm{(tid)}$ is called the tidal deformability and the word “tidal Love number” is reserved for $k_2^\mathrm{(tid)}$.
